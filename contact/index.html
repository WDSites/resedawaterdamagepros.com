Before You Dial

What to Have Ready When You Call

You do not need everything below to call. The more you can tell the dispatcher, the sooner the right crew and equipment can be lined up.

  • The address, with the nearest cross street and whether parking is open for a truck.
  • The source of the water: pipe, appliance, roof, toilet, sewer line or outside flooding.
  • Whether you could stop it, and whether the main water valve is off.
  • Whether power is safe near the water. If you are unsure, say so.
  • Photos or video of the damage, taken before you move anything.
  • Your insurance policy number, if you plan to file a claim.

If there is a gas or electrical risk

Leave the building and call 911 and the utility before calling us. Do not wade through standing water near outlets or an open panel. Switch off breakers only from dry footing at a dry panel.

The Assessment

What an Assessment Covers

An assessment is a look at the loss, not a commitment. The technician finds the source, measures moisture in walls, floors and ceilings with moisture meters, and uses a thermal imaging camera to spot wet areas you cannot see.

From those readings comes a written scope: which rooms are affected, the water category, which materials can be dried and which must come out, and how the price will be built. Take your time reading it before you decide.

If your loss is large or involves insurance, ask for photos and the moisture readings to share with your adjuster. For the full list of work, see our services page, and to check coverage in your area, see our service areas.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I contact you by text or online?

This is a call-only business. Water losses move quickly, and a spoken conversation is the fastest way to share the details that matter.

What if I do not know where the water is coming from?

Call anyway. Say where you see it and what you have already tried. Finding the source is part of the assessment.

Do I need my insurance information before I call?

No. Call first so the water can be dealt with. Have your policy number handy if you have it, and ask your insurer what your policy covers.
