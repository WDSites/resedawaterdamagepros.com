Which Service Fits

Match the Service to the Problem

A water loss moves through stages, and each stage has its own work. Some callers need water out of the house this minute. Others have a dry house with a stained ceiling and want to know what to rebuild. Choosing the right page saves a phone call's worth of explaining, though the dispatcher can sort it out with you either way.

The six services below are grouped by intent. Read the one that sounds like your situation, and the cards underneath take you to the full page.

Right Now

Water Is Standing in the House

If water is pooled on a floor, in a garage or under a sink cabinet, you need emergency water removal. Pumps and extraction units pull the bulk of the water out, and the crew checks what the water reached before it soaks into drywall and subfloor.

Removal is only the first move. Once the pooled water is gone, the structure still holds moisture, which is where water mitigation takes over.

First 24 to 72 Hours

Mitigation: Stop the Damage From Growing

Mitigation covers the first day or three. It includes locating and stopping the source, starting extraction, setting air movers and LGR dehumidifiers, and mapping moisture with meters and a thermal imaging camera. The goal is to limit how much material has to be torn out later.

Think of mitigation as the work that decides the size of the repair bill. Dry framing and subfloor are cheaper to save than to replace, and the window for saving them is short.

After the Water Stops

Cleanup: Removal and Sanitizing

Water damage cleanup is for what the water left behind: soaked carpet padding, swollen baseboards, debris and odor. Wet materials that cannot be dried are cut out and bagged, and the surfaces underneath are cleaned, sanitized and deodorized.

Cleanup matters most when the water was not clean to begin with. Gray water from an appliance and black water from a sewer line need different handling than a supply line leak, and the crew sets the approach after identifying the category.

Storm and Sewage

Flood: Category 2 and 3 Water

Flood damage restoration is the page for storm runoff, groundwater, sewage backup and any water that arrived from outside the plumbing. These losses call for protective gear, tighter removal rules for porous materials and disinfection before rebuilding. Flood is also commonly excluded from standard policies, so read the insurance note on that page.

Rebuild

Repair: Putting the Home Back Together

Once readings confirm the structure is dry, water damage repair replaces drywall and insulation, repairs or refinishes flooring, and repaints. Ceiling stains, wall bubbles and cupped hardwood belong here. Repair is separate from drying and has its own scope and price, explained before the work begins.

Secondary Service

Mold: When Moisture Has Already Done Its Work

Mold remediation is the secondary service on this list because it usually follows a moisture problem that was not dried in time. Mold needs moisture to grow, so the leak or humidity source has to be fixed first. Containment, HEPA air scrubbers and removal follow the S520 standard, and independent clearance testing is a separate step. Questions about health effects belong with a physician.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Which service should I call for first when a pipe bursts?

Start with emergency water removal and water mitigation together. Removal clears the standing water and mitigation sets up drying. Cleanup and repair come after the structure is dry.

Do I need all six services after a leak?

No. A small supply line leak might need only mitigation and repair. A sewage backup may need flood restoration, cleanup and repair. The assessment decides which parts apply.

How is the price for each service set?

Price follows scope: the water category, area, materials and drying time. The scope and price are explained before work starts, and they are set after someone has looked at the loss.
