How to Eliminate Mold in a Basement (or Any Closed Below-Grade Space) in Reseda

Mold in a basement, garage room or windowless closet follows moisture, so the water comes first and the mold second. This guide covers the source hunt, the S520 process and the point where you should stop and call.

Reality check

Many Reseda homes have no basement, and that changes the question

No public source we could find says how many Reseda properties have a basement, so this guide does not guess. If yours does, or you have a sunken garage room, a ground-level utility room with a concrete floor or a storage closet against an exterior wall, the same rules apply to all of them.

What these spaces share is poor drying. They sit against soil or slab, they get little airflow, and stacked boxes keep walls from releasing moisture. Mold needs moisture to grow, so a closed room that stays damp is where trouble usually begins.

If you live in a single-level home or an apartment with nothing below grade, keep reading anyway. The moisture hunt and the containment steps below fit a bathroom wall, a garage with finished drywall or a laundry nook just as well.

Source first

Find the water before you touch the mold

Removing mold while the source keeps feeding it only resets the clock. Callers often tell us about a recent leak: a toilet supply line, a sink drain, an AC condensate line, a roof or a gap in siding. Whatever that leak is, it gets fixed before anyone scrubs or cuts.

Walk the room with a flashlight and look for the usual suspects. Check the water heater fittings, the washing machine hoses, a hose bib that drips inside the wall, a downspout that empties beside the foundation and a sprinkler head aimed at the stucco.

A moisture meter turns a hunch into a number. Drywall or framing that still reads high after the visible water is gone points to a hidden source or incomplete drying. A hygrometer on the shelf also helps, because a basement that sits above EPA's 60 percent humidity ceiling keeps feeding mold.

Reseda, the Sepulveda Basin and what a damp lower room means after a storm

The Army Corps lists Reseda as one of six communities bordering the Sepulveda Basin, and the City Bureau of Engineering describes the basin as a flood management basin of roughly 2,000 acres built into the LA River system. That tells you the area has real flood infrastructure. It does not tell you whether your lot floods, so look up your own address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center before assuming anything either way.

NOAA's 1991 to 2020 normals for Van Nuys Airport put annual rainfall at 13.42 inches, and December through March supplies about 82 percent of it by our arithmetic on those figures. Practically, a closed lower room is most likely to take on seepage or a plumbing surprise in that stretch, so open it up and check it with a meter in spring, not only after a visible leak.

No state licensing regulations for mold remediators exist, according to CDPH's mold FAQ. When you hire for this work, ask for a written scope, ask whether crews follow S520 and keep your own copy of the drying log.

The process

What removal looks like when it follows S520

Mold remediation has a published industry standard, ANSI/IICRC S520, and a fair question for any bidder is whether its crews follow it. The sequence below is the shape of a job done that way.

1

Assess and map

A technician measures moisture with a meter, scans walls with a thermal imaging camera and sketches how far the damage runs.

2

Contain

Plastic sheeting seals the work area, and HEPA air scrubbers hold the room under negative pressure so spores are not pushed into clean rooms.

3

Remove and clean

Porous materials such as wet drywall, carpet and insulation are cut out and bagged. Wood framing is cleaned with HEPA vacuums and damp wiping.

4

Dry and verify

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers bring materials back to normal readings, and the crew records them in a drying log.

5

Clear

A separate assessor can run independent clearance testing, so the company that did the work is not grading its own job.

Your part

What you can handle yourself, and where to stop

Occupants can usually handle a small area, and EPA draws that line near 10 square feet. Wear an N-95 respirator, gloves and goggles, and never paint over mold. Painting hides it and leaves the moisture in place.

Stop and call when the patch is larger, when it keeps returning to the same wall, or when the room took on flood water or sewage. Sewage or other contaminated water is professional work by EPA's reckoning, and EPA also says not to run an HVAC system you suspect is contaminated.

Carpet, ceiling tiles and similar porous items may need to be thrown out after a flood. If water came in from a storm or a backed-up line, the job starts as flood damage restoration and mold work follows once the structure is dry.

Health questions about mold exposure belong to a physician. We can describe the building side only: where the moisture is, what is wet and what has to come out.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Does living near the Sepulveda Basin mean my lower room will flood?

Not by itself. The basin is a flood management project, and its presence says little about one property. A FEMA map search by street address answers the zone question, and your insurer can explain flood coverage, which standard policies usually exclude.

Can I clean basement mold with bleach?

Bleach does little on porous materials such as drywall and carpet, because the growth reaches below the surface. Drywall falls in the porous group that CDPH says rarely cleans up well. Dry wiping or spraying also stirs up spores, so contain first.

Do I need mold testing before removal?

A lab test before cleanup adds little when growth is plain to see or smell, and CDPH does not recommend one. Testing earns its place afterward, as independent clearance testing that checks whether the work met the written scope.

My buyer's lender wants the mold addressed. What do I do?

Ask the lender what documentation it wants, then plan for a written scope, completed remediation and independent clearance results. We cannot promise any lender will accept a given report, so confirm that in writing with them.

How do I know the moisture is gone?

Meter readings on the affected materials should match readings on an unaffected wall in the same room, and they should hold for several days. A drying log with dated readings is your proof.
