When Standing Water Comes In With the Storm

Storm water damage is what happens when rain and runoff arrive at ground level and push into a building through doors, garage openings, vents, window wells and low walls. If water is on your floor now, we answer the 24/7 line, and a few minutes of the right action helps. Stay out of moving or deep water, lift what you can onto furniture or shelves, and keep children and pets out.

A household vacuum is the wrong tool for standing water and adds a shock hazard. Do not walk through water toward the garage or a panel. Photograph the water line on the walls before it drops, because that mark shows how high the water reached.

Safety first

Power Outages and Storm Safety

Outage plus water means treat every outlet as live

Storms often take out power, and it can come back without warning. If water is near outlets, a garage door opener, a sump pump or a panel, keep away and ring 911 or your electric utility first, then us. Flip breakers off only when the panel stands in a dry area and your path to it is dry too.

Never run a generator in a garage or inside the house. Exhaust fumes build up fast. Keep it outdoors and away from doors, windows and vents. If you smell gas, leave and call from outside.

Runoff still coming in?

Stay out of the water if outlets or a panel are nearby, then call the 24/7 line. Tell the dispatcher which door or room is taking water.

How it happens

Why Ground-Level Water Gets In

The National Weather Service defines a flash flood as a rapid and extreme flow of high water into a normally dry area, beginning within six hours of the cause. That description fits a dry Valley street or a driveway that slopes toward the house. If your lot drains toward a door or garage, a short heavy burst is enough to put water inside.

Reseda sits beside the Sepulveda Basin. The Army Corps of Engineers describes Sepulveda Dam as a flood control project that holds upstream runoff and releases it to the Los Angeles River within the downstream channel capacity. That system manages river flow, not the water running off your own lot, so do not assume the basin keeps your doorway dry.

The Basin borders Encino and Tarzana as well, so crews also work Encino and Tarzana after the same storms.

Common weak points are the threshold under a door, a garage floor lower than the driveway, a clogged yard drain, a downspout that dumps against the wall, and soil graded toward the house. Check each one after a storm and note which took water.

What you see

What Storm Water Leaves Behind

Silt line on walls

A tide mark shows how high the water got. Drywall and baseboards below it are usually wet through.

Mud on floors and in carpet

Runoff carries soil, leaves and street residue. It dries into a film that holds moisture and odor.

Swollen baseboards and door casings

Wood trim wicks water upward. Swelling means the wall bottom is wet behind it.

Wet garage with stored boxes

Cardboard soaks fast and stays wet. Move it onto shelves and photograph what was lost.

Damp odor after the water recedes

Moisture trapped under flooring or behind walls keeps going. Moisture meters find it.

Wet insulation at the wall base

Insulation that was submerged does not recover. It comes out with the wet drywall.

Our process

How a Storm Cleanup Runs

Runoff that crosses a yard, street or planter is rarely clean. Under ANSI/IICRC S500 practice, ground-level storm water is generally treated as Category 2 or 3, which means protective gear, careful removal of porous materials and sanitizing, not just drying.

1

Extract and clear

Submersible pumps start the job, with truck-mounted or portable extraction units taking the rest of the standing water. Crews then clear mud and debris off the floor.

2

Map the wet areas

A thermal imaging camera and moisture meters reveal how far water wicked into walls and flooring.

3

Remove what cannot be saved

Soaked pad, baseboards and the bottom of wet drywall come out. Flood cuts are made above the water line when needed. Details are in water damage cleanup.

4

Dry and sanitize

Once surfaces are cleaned and treated, air movers and LGR dehumidifiers pull the moisture out of the framing.

5

Log and document

Daily readings, photos and the drying log are kept for your insurance claim.

Insurance

Storm Damage and Flood Insurance

Standard homeowners and renters policies in California usually leave out flood, and the Department of Insurance fact sheet names flood and mudflow among those exclusions. Rain that blows through a damaged opening may be treated differently. Read your policy and ask your insurer which one applies.

Flood policies typically start only after a 30-day wait, according to LA County storm guidance, so coverage bought once a warning is issued will not respond to that storm. Check floodsmart.gov and the FEMA Flood Map Service Center to see where your address sits. The LA County Public Works Flood Zone Determination site is another way to look it up.

Keep a record from the first hour

Photos of the water line, the entry point and damaged items, plus our moisture readings and drying log, support whatever claim applies. Ask your adjuster before throwing anything out.

Prevention

Before the Next Storm

From June through September, NOAA's 1991-2020 data for Van Nuys Airport averages 0.12 inches or less of rain per month, which makes storm season a short winter window. Use the dry months to get ready. Clear yard drains, check where downspouts discharge, and note which door took water. LA County Public Works also runs a Sandbag Locator for finding sandbags in your area.

Restoration is not waterproofing. Drying and cleanup restore what the water reached, but they do not change the grading or seal a threshold. A landscaper or contractor handles those, and the CSLB advises three bids and a written contract.

For the broader picture, the National Weather Service lists a January and February 1969 flood across central and southern California with 41 deaths and 40 disaster-declared counties. It is a reminder that big winters happen and that a plan beats a scramble.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Is emergency storm water damage cleanup available in Reseda at night or on weekends?

Yes, at any hour. Phones are staffed overnight and on weekends. Staying out of the water and away from outlets is the smartest use of the wait.

Is storm water covered by my homeowners policy?

It depends on how the water got in. Flood is generally excluded and needs separate flood coverage. Rain through a storm-damaged opening may be treated differently. Check your policy and ask your insurer.

Is storm runoff considered contaminated?

Usually, yes. Water that crosses yards and streets picks up soil and bacteria, so it is generally treated as Category 2 or 3 and cleaned with protective gear.

Can you pump out my garage after the storm drains outside?

Yes. Pumps and extraction units clear the water, then we remove mud and dry the slab edges and walls. Remove stored items first if you can do it safely.

Does Sepulveda Dam protect my home from storm water?

It manages river-scale flood flow. It does not control runoff on your own lot or street. Use the FEMA flood map for your address to see what applies.
