Emergency sewage backup in Reseda: what to do while you wait

A sewage backup is an emergency from the first gurgle. Phones are staffed around the clock, 24/7, and dispatch follows your call. Until the crew is scheduled, stop using every toilet, sink, tub, shower, dishwasher and washing machine, because each one sends more water down the same blocked line.

Keep people and pets out of the affected rooms. Do not mop it, do not run a household vacuum over it, and do not flush anything to test the line.

Safety

First-hour safety: stay out and keep the power in check

Before you call anyone

If water is touching outlets, cords, appliances or the electrical panel, do not reach in. Shut off power at the panel only when the breaker box is dry and the walk to it stays out of the water. A gas smell or a damp panel means walking away, phoning 911 or the utility, and only then dialing us.

Treat everything the water touched as contaminated, including shoes and socks. Wash your hands with soap and clean water after any contact.

EPA guidance on cleaning up after water damage says to bring in a professional for sewage or other contaminated water. That is why our crews wear protective gear and bag what comes out, and why you should not try the cleanup with a bucket and a mop.

Backup in one bathroom or the whole house?

Tell the dispatcher which drains are involved and how far the water has spread. That detail helps sort a private line problem from a city main problem.

Who to call first

City sewer main or private line: who owns the problem

The pipe that leaves your building and the larger pipe under the street are not the same pipe. A clog in your own drain line is usually a plumber's job. A problem in the public main under the street belongs to the city. The first call depends on which one failed, and the clues are usually visible.

If there is any chance the public main is involved, make that call early, because a city crew can clear the main and a plumber cannot. Then call us for the cleanup. We do not repair sewer lines.

Why it is different

What Category 3 water means for your floors and walls

Sewage backup water is classed as Category 3, the most contaminated of the three water categories. The category is set by the source, not by how clear the water looks.

That classification drives the plan. Anything porous that the water reached soaks it up and cannot be reliably sanitized. Per EPA guidance, soaked carpet and ceiling tiles can end up in the dumpster once contaminated water touches them, and drywall, insulation and pad are judged the same way. Hard, sealed surfaces can usually be cleaned and stay.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for water damage restoration and sets out the water categories, so it is worth asking whoever you hire if their crew follows it.

Symptoms

Signs the backup is bigger than it looks

Gurgling or bubbling

Air pushed back through a toilet or tub drain when another fixture runs usually means the line is blocked downstream.

Water at the lowest drain

Floor drains, tubs and ground-floor toilets fill first because they sit lowest on the line.

Dark staining on baseboards

Water has wicked up the wall. The drywall behind the paint may be wet well above the visible line.

Odor that returns after cleaning

Contaminated pad, insulation or subfloor is still in place. Surface cleaning does not reach it.

Process

How our crews handle a sewage backup

1

Assess and contain

The technician checks the source, takes moisture readings and photographs the room. Plastic sheeting and a HEPA air scrubber help keep the work area separate from the rest of the home.

2

Extract the water

Truck-mounted or portable extraction units pull standing wastewater out. The waste is handled as contaminated, never rinsed down a household drain.

3

Remove porous materials

Wet carpet, pad, baseboard, insulation and affected drywall are cut out, bagged and carried away. A flood cut above the wicking line is common.

4

Clean and sanitize

Remaining hard surfaces and framing are scrubbed with detergent first, then treated with an antimicrobial product and left for its stated contact time.

5

Dry and verify

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run until a moisture meter shows the framing and subfloor back at normal readings. Drying logs record each visit.

What changes the cost of a sewage cleanup

Any figure quoted sight unseen is a guess. The estimate follows the walk-through, with the scope and cost explained to you prior to any equipment being unpacked. Five things push it up or down.

How far the water spread

One bathroom floor is a smaller job than water that reached hallway carpet and bedrooms.

Materials that must come out

Carpet and pad, drywall height, cabinet bases and insulation each add removal and disposal work.

Depth and volume

More standing water means more extraction before the cleaning even begins.

Access and containment

Sealing off an area and running air scrubbers takes setup time that a simple floor drain overflow may not need.

Drying time

Wet subfloor or wall cavities need more days of equipment than a surface wetting.

Coverage

Sewage backup and your insurance

Backup coverage is often a separate line item

Homeowners and renters policies treat water backup differently from a burst pipe, and flood from outside is usually excluded. According to the California Department of Insurance, a renters policy may respond to water backup damage, so read your wording or put the question to your insurer. Photograph everything before it is removed, and keep the plumber's invoice and our drying logs.

Renters and landlords

If you rent or live in a multi-unit building

Tell the landlord or property manager in writing the same day, with photos. The California Attorney General's habitability sheet says landlords must keep plumbing working, so a backup from a shared building line is not yours to fix. For unsafe conditions at a City of Los Angeles rental, the Housing Department takes code violation reports on 1-866-557-7368.

Avoid

Mistakes to avoid after a backup

  • Running water to see whether the line has cleared. It can push more waste into the house.
  • Using a wet vacuum made for household use. It can spray contaminated water into the air and is hard to clean afterwards.
  • Keeping the carpet because it looks dry on top. The pad underneath holds the water.

Once the house is stable, a plumber can camera the line and tell you what failed. Drying and rebuilding come afterward, and our water damage repair page explains that stage. Crews also serve nearby homes in Tarzana and Northridge.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Is emergency sewage backup cleanup available in Reseda at night or on weekends?

Yes. Someone picks up at every hour, weekends and holidays included, and a crew is dispatched once you have called. Arrival times cannot be promised in advance.

Should I call the city or a plumber first?

If several drains or neighbors are affected, or you see overflow at a street manhole, call LA Sanitation's 24-hour line first. If it is one fixture, call a plumber. Then call us for the cleanup in either case.

Can I clean up a small toilet overflow myself?

If the water was only a toilet overflow with no waste, some people handle a small area with gloves and boots. If it came up from a drain or has waste in it, EPA says to hire a professional.

Will you replace my carpet and drywall?

We remove the affected material. Rebuilding and new flooring are a separate stage, and our repair page explains how that works.

Does a Reseda renter need to do anything different?

Report it to the landlord in writing the same day, photograph the damage and ask your renters insurer what documentation it wants. Ask whether the failed line is private or shared, since that decides who arranges the plumbing.
